\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Statistical scope.}
All mission findings concern 11 validation events from six synchronized channels
on a mission already used for development. Correlated frames, PCA ranks, budget
caps, and repeated seeds do not increase the number of independent faults.
The adjacent-pair policy was designed after observing a temporal validation
failure; its eight-arm result is exploratory. A frozen independent-mission
assessment is the next test of generalization. The available transfer interface
has been exercised on synthetic bundles only.

\paragraph{Receiver and diagnostic scope.}
The pointwise receivers share normalization, and the AR(1) receiver is a simple
linear model. Evidence under these models does not establish compatibility with
arbitrary future detectors. Event coverage and raw context are not validated
root-cause explanations or measures of operator usefulness. Summary extrema can
associate unrelated observations with an event. Stronger temporal receivers,
established adaptive-retention competitors, and engineering assessment are needed
before asserting broader diagnostic benefit or superiority over prior methods.

\paragraph{Recorder design.}
Background phase is deterministic, context lengths are counted in samples rather
than elapsed time, and recorder state resets at epoch boundaries. Trigger capacity
is spent chronologically rather than through an optimized eviction rule. Pairing
trades temporal spread for consecutive inputs and can leave odd quota slots unused.
Single-component removals do not identify interactions or optimal allocations.
The current schema requires six synchronized channels; asynchronous observations
and arbitrary channel counts require explicit source and format extensions.

\paragraph{Resource and delivery scope.}
The application-byte contract excludes physical link framing. The separate
simulator charges illustrative overhead and finite-buffer contacts, but its
schedule lacks mission-specific requirements and its single loss seed does not
support an uncertainty estimate. Partial wire progress abstracts physical frame
timing; retransmission is not modeled. Congestion comparisons also reflect
unequal actual expenditure under shared caps. Python-traced allocations and
single-run host throughput do not establish total memory, embedded latency, or
flight suitability. A compiled implementation would require a measured deployment
need rather than serving as evidence of one.
