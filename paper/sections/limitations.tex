\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Next Evidence}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{What is supported.}
Mission1 shows that an unchanged event count can conceal lost pre-event context,
and that pointwise evidence can survive while temporal input requirements fail.
The paired control changes the available transition structure under the same cap;
the Mission2 follow-up reproduces that structural distinction. These observations
support evaluating the retained inputs required by a receiver, not ranking a
recorder solely by the anomaly score that selected its samples.

\paragraph{What is not established.}
The Mission1 event findings remain development results on 11 anomalies. The pair
control was designed after inspecting them. Mission2 has no eligible anomaly
labels in the fixed source subset and therefore cannot confirm or refute an event-
recovery improvement. Its millions of observations and completed arm count do not
repair that empty denominator. Correlated frames, repeated seeds, caps, and PCA
ranks are not independent faults. We report descriptive paired counts and observed
seed ranges without treating them as generalization confidence intervals.

\paragraph{Data qualification and scope.}
The six-channel synchronized interface constrained source selection, and metadata
alone failed to establish label support. A future protocol should qualify anomaly
coverage independently of algorithm scores or cover the complete intended target
set with an explicit asynchronous/variable-width representation. The present
attempt remains reported rather than being replaced after outcomes were known.
Both sources belong to the same curated ESA collection, with anonymized identities,
units, and timelines. Released ground telemetry is not evidence that every original
onboard observation was available, and archive replay does not validate a physical
acquisition or downlink system. No result establishes root-cause diagnosis or
operator usefulness.

\paragraph{Method limitations.}
Calibration assumes that annotated training exclusions are available; operational
training quality may differ. The pointwise receiver bank shares normalization,
and AR(1) is a simple linear
model selected for its consecutive-input requirement. The disabled trigger is a
controlled stress case, not an estimated probability of onboard failure. Two-sample
bursts are tailored to that requirement and do not protect arbitrary forecasting
windows or unknown future models. Background phase is deterministic, context is
counted in samples, and recorder state resets at epoch boundaries. Capacity is
spent chronologically rather than through an optimized trigger-record eviction
policy. Single removals do not establish interactions or an optimal allocation.

\paragraph{Resources and comparison.}
Shared recording caps permit unequal actual expenditure. The contact schedules
are illustrative; partial wire progress is not a physical radio model, and one
loss seed supports no uncertainty claim. Temporal availability is measured before
delivery. Host throughput and traced allocations do not establish embedded RAM,
latency, or flight suitability. Learned codecs, stronger temporal models, and
established adaptive-retention methods remain direct competitors not implemented
in this comparison. An annotation-supported frozen evaluation, engineering link
requirements, and operator assessment are needed before broader performance or
deployment claims.
